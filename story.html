Three appraisers. Three different weeks.

These are sketches of the working situations the desk is built for, not customer testimonials. See if one of them sounds like yours.

The first appraiser

Joe's team can inspect more than it can finish

Joe is a Tennessee appraiser with forty years behind him. Booms, crashes, refinance waves, years when the phone wouldn't stop. He built a respected shop with a team that runs from trainees to senior staff, and on busy days the inspections end with more reports open than anyone can finish.

Joe isn't short on work and he isn't short on know-how. He's buried. His team can inspect the properties, understand the markets, and make the appraisal decisions. The reports just aren't written yet.

He could hire, but that means recruiting, training, payroll, and overhead he keeps paying when the market cools off. Instead his team keeps inspecting and deciding, and the next morning his staff starts from finished drafts instead of blank forms. They go through them, fix what needs fixing, sign, and deliver.

The second appraiser

Harry wants to stop writing reports at night

Harry is an old-school appraiser. He likes driving a neighborhood and noticing how one street differs from the next. He likes walking the property and seeing what never shows up in a database: traffic noise, renovation quality, lot utility, the subtle difference between two homes that look identical on paper. To Harry, that part of the work is the art.

What he doesn't enjoy is getting home after a full day of inspections and realizing the workday is only half over. Room counts, photo organizing, record pulling, the same commentary he's written thousands of times. He knows how to do all of it. He just doesn't want it eating his life.

Now Harry finishes his inspections, sends in the assignment materials, and steps away. While he's at dinner with his family, I'm writing and checking his report. The next morning he comes back to the part that matters, reviews the analysis, makes the final calls, and stands behind the appraisal.

The third appraiser

John needs help without becoming a manager

John is independent, and he likes it that way. No staff meetings, no payroll, no one asking him questions all day. He trusts his own judgment and controls every file from beginning to end.

That works until the orders pile up. Monday he's caught up. Then five assignments arrive, four more on Tuesday, and a good client asks about a rush. Saying yes pays, and it also eats another evening or a chunk of his weekend. He doesn't want an assistant whose livelihood rides on a market that rises and falls, and training one might take longer than doing the work himself. But he's reached the limit of what one person can produce.

This desk is his third option. He keeps his clients, his conclusions, and most of his fee, and gets the output of a bigger shop without managing anyone.

Where this fits

If one of those weeks looked familiar, we should talk.

  • More orders than you can comfortably produce
  • Evenings and weekends going to report writing
  • No interest in hiring and managing staff
  • You want an editable draft and organized sources back, not new software to learn

You inspect and send the assignment materials. I organize the file, write the report, and check it twice, once against the source data and once reading it through the way a reviewer would. I use modern software in that work, including AI, and I run and review every file myself, with the assistance disclosed in every report. A finished draft is back to you the next morning. You fix what needs fixing, make every final appraisal decision, sign, and deliver. The Production Desk page covers the tools, data handling, and disclosure in plain terms.

What you keep

  • Your clients and your fee
  • Your comps, your adjustments, your opinion of value
  • The final say on every page, and the signature
  • Your independence

Pay per report, flat, quoted directly. Stop anytime.

I'm keeping this small on purpose.

I prepare and review each desk file myself, and I don't farm your files out to anybody. I take up to five standard files per business day, and when the day is full, I tell you before you send. I built this because I didn't want to choose between growing a business and having a life outside it either.
