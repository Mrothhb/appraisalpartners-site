In Thursday evening. On your desk Friday afternoon.

Finish your inspections, send the order and your appraiser data: photos, sketch, and notes. What goes in Thursday evening is back as a complete UAD 3.6 draft, ready for your review, Friday afternoon. Usually sooner.

You inspect the property and make every appraisal decision. I write the report and build the supporting file, with my assistance disclosed in the certification. You go through it, fix what needs fixing, sign, and deliver. I do the work myself, in Texas. Nothing is farmed out to another person.

The point is a smooth UAD 3.6 transition. Beginning November 2, 2026, new appraisal reports submitted to UCDP must use UAD 3.6. I write the new form every day, so your practice doesn't have to learn it under deadline pressure and your pipeline doesn't slow down while everyone adjusts.

I hold the desk to what I can personally produce and review, and when the day is full, I tell you before you send. Committed delivery time is confirmed when I accept each order.

The Production Desk is a distinct service line of Appraisal Partners. It has its own client relationships, scope, onboarding, and written terms, separate from my residential appraisal practice. This is contract report-production assistance; I do not join your staff or sign your report. AMC and lender programs use their own vendor approval and written agreement. If your AMC or lending program permits named significant appraisal assistance, we can discuss a limited arrangement for participating signing appraisers.

The responsibility line

The signing appraiser retains

  • Client and engagement relationship
  • Inspection and inspection findings
  • Scope and final comparable selection
  • Final adjustments and appraisal judgments
  • Reconciliation and opinion of value
  • Certification, signature, and delivery

Appraisal Partners produces

  • Authorized MLS and public-record research
  • Comparable candidates and proposed analyses
  • Report data and narrative
  • Maps, exhibits, and organized source material
  • Consistency and completion checks
  • An editable draft report and supporting file

A narrower draft-from-your-research scope can also be arranged when your office already has the comps, adjustments, and market data assembled.

How a file moves

  1. 1.We establish the relationship first. Onboarding covers your markets, client mix, engagement restrictions, expected volume, authorized data access, software and deliverables, schedule, and written terms.
  2. 2.You forward the order, inspect, and send the packet by end of day. Photos, sketch, and written or voice notes, through the agreed secure channel.
  3. 3.I produce the desk side of the assignment. Research through the agreed sources, comparable candidates and proposed analyses, and the complete report, checked twice: against the source data, then read through the way a reviewer would.
  4. 4.You review it as the appraiser. The draft is back on your desk the next afternoon, usually sooner. Accept, change, or reject anything in it. The final judgments, the signature, and the delivery are yours.

The deliverable: an editable working file in the agreed software format, applicable PDF and UAD output, organized supporting materials, and a short record of assumptions or unresolved items that deserve attention during review. I fill the report as completely as the evidence allows and flag anything you need to verify or decide.

Markets and current boundaries

Designed for

  • Established residential appraisers and firms
  • AMC or lender arrangements that permit named assistance
  • Recurring UAD 3.6 production needs

Current boundaries

  • Texas and Colorado are the starting markets
  • Other markets require client, licensing, and data-access review
  • Current scope is conventional and private residential work
  • No shared MLS credentials, in either direction

Market setup is deliberate. Client permission, licensing, market competence, and authorized MLS and data access are confirmed before production begins; a new market may take one to three weeks to establish.

Why I am offering this

I've spent twenty years in residential appraisal, and I've always gravitated to the desk side: the research, the analysis, the report writing itself. I want a few stable working relationships where the division of work is clear, the signing appraiser stays in control, and the client receives credible work. UAD 3.6 makes that help useful now, but it is not a reason to cut corners or hide who did what. Capacity and delivery timing are agreed before the first file and kept at a level I can personally review.

Disclosure, data, and trust

The certification identifies me and describes the assistance provided. When the work is significant real property appraisal assistance, that disclosure is not optional, and I do not remove it. Client, jurisdiction, and licensing requirements are confirmed before an assignment is accepted.

Files move through a secure channel we agree on during onboarding. I don't farm your files out to anybody. No client is solicited, no MLS login is shared, and assignment data is never sold.

I use software automation and AI-assisted tools in this work, and yes, that includes the drafting. I direct and review every production file myself; the signing appraiser independently reviews and owns the appraisal judgments. Before live work begins, we identify the software and service providers expected to handle assignment data, along with their available storage, retention, and data-use terms, so you and your client can approve the setup.
