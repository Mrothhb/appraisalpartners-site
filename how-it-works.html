Comps and direction

At intake you pick one of three ways to run the comparables:

  1. 1.Use my comps. You pulled them, you know the market. I build from your selections and your direction.
  2. 2.Prepare candidates and proceed. I research the pool, document what was considered, and build the grid from the strongest candidates. You judge the selections at review.
  3. 3.Candidate memo first. Before the grid is built, you get a short memo: the pool, the candidates, and why. Reply with direction, or let it proceed as proposed after the stated window. Silence lets production continue; it never means you've adopted the comps. That adoption happens at your review, like always.

Steering means facts and evidence: corrections, include-or-exclude direction with your reasoning, additional market data, a documented change in scope. It never means steering toward a number. If an instruction reads like value pressure, I'll say so and decline it.

When something needs fixing

Four kinds of fixes, four plain answers. The rule behind all of them: I handle the mechanical production work; your job is independent appraisal judgment.

  • My error, my fix. A transposed number, a mis-mapped field, a missing exhibit, a file that won't validate. Free, first in line, no time limit, no count limit. My errors are mine.
  • Your direction. You reviewed the draft and want it different: swap a comp for your preferred candidate, reweight the reconciliation, reword the commentary. One consolidated, within-scope round is included with every accepted trial or live report. Send it as one list, the way a lender sends conditions, and I confirm a return time before starting.
  • The assignment changed. Amended contract, re-measured GLA, a new intended user. I confirm new timing first, and if it means real rework, you get a quote before anything happens. You can always say no.
  • Your client kicks it back. Underwriter conditions after you've delivered? Send them over. Anything that traces to my production work is fixed free and first in line, for thirty days after delivery.

If a paid package materially misses something in the accepted scope and I cannot correct it through the included correction process, you do not pay for that report. The terms define that line so neither of us has to guess.
