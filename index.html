How a report moves

  1. 1.Start with one report. Fill out a short form. You get an order number and a private folder link back, usually within business hours. No call, no sales conversation.
  2. 2.Send the packet. The order, photos, sketch, your notes, the contract if it's a purchase. The start page shows exactly what to send for your kind of assignment, so nothing gets forgotten.
  3. 3.Get a firm acceptance. Received is not accepted. I confirm scope, a flat price, and a committed return time before production begins. I hold the desk to what I can personally produce and review, and when the day is full, I tell you before you send.
  4. 4.Watch it move. Every order has its own status page: where the report is, what happened last, what happens next, and when it's due back. Optional email or text updates at the moments that matter.
  5. 5.Review, revise, sign, deliver. The draft is yours. Accept, change, or reject anything in it. One consolidated direction round is included, and my errors are fixed free, always.

You can see where your report is

Most production help is a black box: you send an order into the dark and hope it comes back. This desk isn't built that way. Every order gets its own status page, no login needed, that answers the questions you'd actually ask. Where is it now? What happened last? What happens next? When is it due back on my desk?

If I need something from you, the page says so and you get a ping. If you'd rather not check at all, opt into email or text updates when the report is accepted, when I need something, and when the draft is ready. And the page never shows your client's name, the property address, or any number from the report. Your report is your business.

What comes back

An editable draft, not a printout. If you're on TOTAL, you get the native working file, ready to open in your own software with everything editable and the photos placed. On other software, you get the UAD 3.6 package: the XML, a draft-watermarked PDF, the exhibits, and the organized support file. Tell me your software and version at intake and I confirm compatibility at acceptance, not at delivery.

Every draft ships with a short list of open items worth your attention during review, the documented comp pool behind the grid, and suggested assistance-disclosure language. It arrives marked DRAFT and stays a draft until you've made it yours.

The line that never moves

  • Your client stays yours. I never solicit your clients.
  • You keep every final judgment: the comps, the adjustments, the opinion of value, the signature.
  • My assistance is named in the certification. That disclosure is not optional, and I do not remove it.
  • No shared MLS credentials, in either direction.
  • I don't farm your files out to anybody.
