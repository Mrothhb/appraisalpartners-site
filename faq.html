The questions worth asking first.

Short answers here, longer ones on the how it works page. If yours isn't covered, write me and I'll answer it directly.

Whose client is it?

Yours. Always. The engagement is yours, the relationship is yours, the fee is yours. I work for you, not for your client, and I never solicit your clients. The desk only exists because that line holds.

Who's responsible for the appraisal?

You are, and nothing about this service changes that. You keep scope, final comp selection, adjustments, reconciliation, the opinion of value, the certification, and the signature. I produce the draft and the support file for your independent review. You accept, change, or reject anything in it.

Is this allowed? How does disclosure work?

Named significant appraisal assistance is a normal, USPAP-recognized part of the profession when it's disclosed. My name and the work I performed go in the certification, and I don't remove that disclosure for anyone. UAD 3.6 even gives it a dedicated place on the form. Whether your client permits outside assistance is confirmed before I accept the report, not after.

Do you use AI?

Yes, and it's explained once and plainly in the disclosure section: automation and AI-assisted tools help produce the draft, I direct and review every report myself, and you own every appraisal judgment. The tools make the production faster. They don't make the decisions.

What software do you work with?

I produce in TOTAL. If you're on TOTAL, you get the native working file, editable in your own install with photos placed. On other software, you get the UAD 3.6 XML, a draft-watermarked PDF, the exhibits, and the organized support package. Tell me your product and version at intake and compatibility is confirmed at acceptance.

How fast is it, really?

Every report gets a committed return time before production begins, and that's the number that matters. A typical complete packet runs on the shape of in Thursday evening, on your desk Friday afternoon. If a commitment is ever at risk, you hear it from me before the deadline, not after.

What if I don't like the comps?

You steer. At intake you choose: use your comps, let me prepare candidates and judge them at review, or get a candidate memo before the grid is built. Steering means evidence and direction with reasons. It never means steering toward a number, and I'll decline an instruction that reads like value pressure.

What if something's wrong with the draft?

My errors are fixed free, first in line, with no time or count limit. Your direction gets one consolidated revision round included per report. If your underwriter kicks the report back within thirty days, conditions that trace to my production work are also mine, free. And if the draft fundamentally isn't usable to you, you don't pay for it.

What does it cost?

Flat, printed, per report: $89 when you supply the packet, comps, and data. $149 when I run the research on an eligible DFW assignment. Pilot rates. The price confirmed at acceptance is the price you pay, complexity gets flagged before acceptance, and there's no subscription. Stop anytime.

How much can you take on?

Less than a mill, on purpose. I hold the desk to what I can personally produce and review, and when the day is full, I tell you before you send. I don't farm your files out to anybody, so capacity is honest and so are the return times.

Where do you work?

Full research and draft production in Dallas–Fort Worth, where my MLS access and market competence live. Reports where you supply the data are reviewed individually, and the checks are real: client permission, property-state requirements, competence, insurance, data rights, software. The demonstration report is there for anyone, anywhere, first.

What about my client's confidential information?

Keep it out of the first form entirely. Documents move through your private order folder, my status pages never show client names, addresses, or report numbers, and assignment data is never sold or shared. If you want to judge the work before sharing anything at all, that's exactly what the demonstration report is for. We also confirm you're authorized to engage assistance on the report before anything starts.

How do I start?

One report. The start page takes about two minutes, you get an order number and folder back, and the first draft tells you more than any website can. No call, no contract past that report.

Didn't find yours?

Write me at mrr@appraisalpartners.net. I read every note myself and reply directly.
