Where the practice came from

My father, Tim Roth, MAI, SRA, built the California practice where I trained from trainee through licensure. His standard was simple: define the appraisal problem before opening the form. The form is the delivery vehicle; the appraisal is the analysis behind it.

That standard still shapes my work. I remain accountable for the assignments I accept and the conclusions I sign.

Why I built the desk

I’ve run appraisal practices of my own for years, under different companies and in different markets. I’ve managed AMCs, estate attorneys & private clients; answered calls, quoted assignments, scheduled inspections, walked and measured properties, taken the photos & driven the comps. I know how to do all of it.

What I love most begins when the information reaches the desk. I like hunting down missing data, working through paired sales, testing what the market is saying & watching the pieces snap into place. Then comes the writing: explaining the what, why, where & how clearly enough that the conclusion makes sense to someone who was not there.

That is why I chose this desk. You keep the client relationship, inspection & final judgment. I focus on the part of appraisal I do best and enjoy most, while personally reviewing every report. That’s why this is a desk fee, not an appraisal fee.

Why the desk stays small

I only take the reports I can personally complete and review. I want a few durable relationships with appraisers whose work and handoff I know. When the day is full, I say so before you upload anything.
